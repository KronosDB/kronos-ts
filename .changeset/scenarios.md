"@kronos-ts/test": minor
---

Tests run against the app you built. `fixture(parts)` replaces `testFixture`, scenarios are written in the shapes an event model is written in, and assertions are plain functions of the outcome.

- A test composes its app the way a process does — `kronos({ … })` over entries carrying their stores and buses — wraps in recorders what the fixture should observe, and hands both over: `fixture({ app, eventStore, commandBus, queryBus, clock })`. `eventStore` is a `recordingEventStore(…)`, `commandBus` a `recordingCommandBus(…)`; `queryBus` is needed for a query act, `clock` (an `advanceableClock()`) for `advance`, which also needs `controllableSchedulingEventStore(…, clock)` under the recorder. Anything else in the parts, such as a pool, reaches assertions on `outcome.app`, and a `close` is called once the scenario is judged. The fixture builds nothing; a recorder it needs and was not handed is named in the error.
- `fixture(parts).given(...events).when(act).then(...assertions)` runs the scenario and resolves with its `Outcome`. A fixture is one world: `.and` continues it for the next step (`.then(...).and.when(...).then(...)`), which replaces consecutive `run` calls; build a fresh app and fixture per scenario. `fixture()` also takes a promise of the parts.
- `given` events now reach projections. They are handed to the app's processors as a replay (`ctx.isReplay()` is true) while the recorders hold: a command an event handler dispatches is not sent (`send` resolves to `undefined`) and a deadline it arms is not armed. Before, given events skipped every processor, so a read model could only be tested through events arriving.
- `fixture(parts).storyline(query(Q, payload)).shows(expected).after(...events).shows(expected)` walks one read model through its states, each judged once the events before it are handled.
- An assertion is `(outcome) => void | Promise<void>`: it returns when the claim holds and throws when it does not. `Outcome` has `act`, `result`, `threw`, `error`, `events`, `commands`, `schedules` and `app` (the parts). The built-ins are such functions: `events(...)`, `noEvents()`, `commands(...)`, `noCommands()`, `result(value)`, `error(matcher)`, `scheduled(...)` and `cancelled(...)`. `then(event(A), command(C))` becomes `then(events(event(A)), commands(command(C)))`. A function claims a throw by carrying `claimsError: true`, as `error()` does.
- `then` reports every failing claim, not only the first. `.await(...)` is gone: pass `within` in the parts to re-judge claims until they hold.
- `recordingEventStore` and `recordingCommandBus` gain `hold(on)`, and `recordingEventStore` keeps the getters of the store it wraps live (a controllable scheduler's `schedules`, say) instead of reading them once.
- Removed: `testFixture`, `TestFixture`, `RunOutcome`, the free-standing `given(...)` and `scenario()`, the `Scenario` value, `Judgement`, `FixtureOptions`, `FixtureLists`, `FixtureResources`, `FixtureScope`, `FixtureInfrastructure`, `InfrastructureFactory`, `FixtureEventHandler`, `FixtureUnitOfWork`, `PartialProcessor`, `IfAdvanceable` and `ClockOf`. `.advance(...)` is no longer refused at compile time; it is refused when the scenario runs.
